Services

Pest and vermin control, Gippsland and regional Victoria

Every job is planned, insured and carried out humanely under the relevant Victorian codes of practice. Deer control on farms and kangaroo culling are the two most common call-outs; the rest of the list is below.

Deer control12

Sambar and fallow deer hammering pasture, fences, crops and tree plantings. Thermal-assisted night work, planned around your stock and your neighbours.

  • Damage assessment on the first visit
  • Night and thermal work where deer move
  • Carcass handling as agreed with you

Kangaroo culling5

Where numbers are hurting pasture, fences and water. Carried out under a valid Authority to Control Wildlife and the relevant code.

  • Help with the ATCW paperwork
  • Counts and a plan before any work
  • Humane dispatch to the code, every animal

Feral pigs

Dug-up paddocks, fouled dams and crop losses. Pigs move at night, so most pig work is thermal.

  • Sign and camera assessment
  • Night work around your stock
  • Follow-up visits as numbers drop

Foxes3

Lamb and poultry losses, especially around lambing. Timed to the season and your stock.

  • Pre-lambing programs
  • Night spotlight and thermal work
  • Reporting on what was taken

Rabbits4

Warrens under the fence line and chewed-out pasture. Control planned alongside your own warren work.

  • Warren mapping
  • Shooting as part of a wider program
  • Advice on ripping and follow-up

Wild dogs

Stock attacks and losses on the bush edge. Planned with you and your neighbours where it helps.

  • Track and camera assessment
  • Night and thermal work
  • Coordination across boundaries

Night work and thermal

Most feral animals move after dark. Thermal optics let me find them, count them and take them cleanly without lighting up the whole paddock.

  • Thermal spotting and shooting
  • Quiet, planned movements around stock
  • Counts you can use for permits and planning

Thermal drone services optional, remove if not offering yet

Thermal drone surveys to count animals, find mobs and map where they move before a program starts.

  • Pre-program counts
  • Finding stock after a storm or fire
  • Mapping movement corridors

Notes

1Sambar. Introduced to Victoria in the 1860s; now the main deer species across Gippsland's bush edge.

2Fallow. In Australia since the 1830s; paddock and plantation damage, often in daylight.

3Foxes. Released in Victoria in the 1850s for hunting; lambing losses follow the season.

4Rabbits. Twenty-four released at Barwon Park, Winchelsea, in 1859. The rest is history.

5ATCW. Authority to Control Wildlife, issued by the Victorian government under the Wildlife Act 1975.

6Game Licence. Issued by the Game Management Authority; deer are a game species in Victoria.

What a job includes

  • A site visit and a written plan: stock, neighbours, access, dates and safety.
  • Humane dispatch to AHCPMG304 and the code for each species.
  • Carcass handling and disposal as agreed with you.
  • A short report afterwards and a follow-up if the numbers call for it.

Permits and codes, in plain language

Kangaroo control on private land needs an Authority to Control Wildlife5 from the Victorian government; I can help with the application. Deer are a game species and are taken under a Game Licence6. All work follows the relevant Victorian codes of practice for the humane control of the species involved.

Do you charge for the first visit?

Pricing to be confirmed by Fitz. The honest default is to state the call-out and the hourly or per-night rate here, before anyone picks up the phone.

Will you work around my stock and neighbours?

Yes. That is what the site visit is for. Safe lines of fire, stock movements and neighbour notice are agreed before the first night.

What happens to the carcasses?

Handled and disposed of the way we agree in the plan. Some landholders want them removed, some want them left in a pit or a gully; both are fine.
